Glossary

Every word this handbook uses, defined like a friend would. Cross-links hop to the chapter where the word earned its definition.

Batch — Doing one task for several items in a row instead of doing the whole task per item. The reason ten listings take ninety minutes.

Bundle / lot — Several related items sold as one listing. The companion Resale Lot Builder tool forecasts whether a lot nets more than selling pieces (what sells).

Buyer pays for shipping — The listing passes the label price through to the buyer. Note the fee on it still applies.

Calculated shipping — The marketplace estimates label cost from weight and box size to charge the buyer. Requires your scale to be truthful.

Completed/sold listings — Sales that actually happened, as opposed to asking prices, which are wishes. Your only honest pricing data.

Cost of goods (item cost) — What you paid for the item. May be $0 for things sourced from your own house — a legal, honest exception, not a magic trick (sourcing).

Estimated taxes — Occasional quarterly income-tax payments some side-income earners are asked to send. Round version: most small profits land in the once-a-year range; confirm specifics with a professional.

Fee-think — Doing rounded profit math before buying, instead of after. The whole point of the worksheet.

Feedback / review score — The marketplace's grade of you. Protect it with fast refunds, calm replies, and the serious habit of honest describing (photos and listings).

Final value fee — The marketplace's percentage cut of everything the buyer paid, including shipping. Roughly 13-15% in round numbers on eBay (fees).

Flaw photo — A close, well-lit photo of the item's worst feature, taken before the buyer asks. The single strongest scam-prevention move available to a seller.

Floor (walk-away price) — The lowest price you'll accept: item cost + fees + label. Below the floor, selling is a donation.

Free shipping — A listing that rolls the label into the price. Note that it is free to the buyer, not free to the seller: the fee rides the whole amount.

Hazmat rules — Postal-law fact that batteries, aerosols, some liquids and "energy" items can't be shipped casually. Beginner rule: no batteries, no liquids; read up before you graduate.

Inventory — Things you own that you intend to sell. In records terms: every row without a "sold" yet.

Lot — See bundle / lot.

Markdown clock — Your written rule for when a listing gets cheaper: day 15 −10-15%, day 45 to floor (listings).

Marketplace — eBay, Amazon, and similar. The room you sell in; it keeps a cut, collects buyers' sales tax, and runs the protection system you lean on in scam-proofing.

Off-platform payment — Being paid outside the marketplace (check, wire, direct app). The structure of most scams: no protection, no fee math, no recourse. Never.

Offer — A buyer's proposed price. Shopping behavior, not an insult; counter above your floor.

Padded envelope — The flat, quilted mailer. The cheapest good shipping for small flat things, and the reason books are beginner-friendly.

Phishing — A fake email pretending to be a marketplace. Rule of the whole category: don't click links in it; go to the site directly.

Profit — sale price − item cost − fees − shipping. The only word tax authorities care about, and the only number your records exist to prove.

Referral fee — Amazon's name for its category percentage cut (rounding: about 8-15% in many categories). Stackier than eBay; see fees.

Reprice / relist — Editing the price of an aging listing, or re-publishing it to refresh its visibility, per the markdown clock.

Returns — The buyer sends it back. Part-time play: accept them calmly, refund on arrival, keep reviews whole.

Sales tax — The buyer's tax on a purchase. Round-number comfort: big marketplaces collect and remit it for you in most places. (Not the same as income tax.)

Shipping station — Your one corner holding scale, tape, and a small box bank. A tidy station is the difference between chore and drama.

Sold listings — See completed/sold listings.

Time away (vacation mode) — The marketplace's banner and pause settings. Also the politest way to step back for a while — and central to a tidy exit (quitting politely).

Tracking — The scan history of a package. Your primary evidence in "item not received" disputes — one of many reasons labels belong in-app.

Walk-away price — See floor.

Wind-down — A four-to-six-week, no-new-purchases glide to a stopped state, as written in quitting politely.
