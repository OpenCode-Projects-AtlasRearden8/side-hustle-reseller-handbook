Start Here

Welcome. Maybe you have a garage shelf of thingamabobs and a browser tab that says "is eBay a good side hustle?" open at all times. This handbook was written for you — with zero judgment attached. It's about part-time reselling: buying things (or finding them), selling them on eBay or Amazon, and keeping the whole operation small enough that it stays fun.

The promise of this book

By the end, you'll be able to answer these questions in your own words:

  1. What should I actually sell first? (Short version: small, common, easy-to-ship, boring.)
  2. How do I find inventory without spending money I'll regret? (Thrift, clearance, and your own closets — with a hard spending rule.)
  3. How do I list things well with just my phone? (Photos, titles, and honesty — in about ten minutes per item.)
  4. How do I ship things without a panic attack? (One box drawer, one scale, one drop-off routine.)
  5. What about fees and taxes? (In plain round numbers, not spreadsheets that fight back.)
  6. How do I spot a scam before it spots me? (A short list of red flags that covers most of them.)
  7. **And — being fully honest — how do I quit politely if it stops being fun?** (Because it's a side hustle, not a life sentence.)

You will not need a warehouse, a pallet partner, a 40-page business plan, or a single dollar of borrowed money for any of it.

How to read it

The chapters build on each other, but each one stands on its own too. Honest shortcuts:

  • "Is this even worth trying?" Read What sells and the Fee-think worksheet.
  • "I want to list my first thing today, no more reading." Read Photos and listings, then follow the Starter checklist.
  • "Money questions." Fees and taxes and Keeping records.
  • "I'm feeling nervous." Scam-proofing first.
  • "I think I'm done." Quitting politely — no shame, real plan.

The ground rules we wrote by

Three promises, kept on every page:

  1. No condescension. If a word needs defining, we define it the first time (see the Glossary). If a step sounds obvious, we say it anyway — because "obvious" is only obvious once you know it.
  2. Round numbers, clearly flagged. Fee and tax figures here are rounded on purpose so you can do them in your head. When you need exact math, that's what tools are for.
  3. Low stakes first. We start with items where even a mistake is boring: a five-dollar book that ships in a padded envelope. You graduate to bigger things only when you feel ready.

This book is the judgment. The tools do the math.

Two companion tools from Asdale Dynamics, LLC pair naturally with this handbook, and this book deliberately does not repeat their jobs:

  • Resale Lot Builder — turns an itemized CSV of your stash into bundle suggestions and profit forecasts for lots. If this handbook ends with you holding a shoebox of related items, that tool is how you finish the job.
  • Shipping-Cost Comparer (CLI) — compares carrier rates for a given weight, size, and destination. This book teaches shipping judgment (when, what, and where); that tool computes which rate wins.

Use whichever one you need; nothing here requires them, and nothing here duplicates them.

What this handbook is not

  • It is not tax, legal, or financial advice. It uses **round numbers on purpose** — the "about" service, not the "exact" service. When a decision gets real (money, legal, health), a real professional belongs in the conversation.
  • It is not a get-rich scheme, and it will not promise that numbers go up. Reselling is a real job with real hours. We just want the hours to be calm.
  • It is not finished. Marketplaces change; this book is versioned, updated, and open source under the MIT license. Corrections welcome.

The one-paragraph version of the whole book

Sell small, common things you understand. Pay for inventory with cash you already had. $1 of scale beats $1 of guessing. Photograph on a plain background with window light and describe flaws before the buyer finds them. Ship in a flat, padded, well-taped package with your scale's number as truth. Keep about a third of every profit aside in an envelope marked TAXES (rudy math: sale − cost − fees − shipping = taxable profit). Keep one simple list per week in a spreadsheet or notebook, and back it up twice a year (that's your record-keeping done). Decline anything that smells like an overpay stranger in a hurry. And the moment the fun is gone for three therapy-not-boring weeks in a row, quit politely, on your own terms, with your records intact and your name in good standing.
