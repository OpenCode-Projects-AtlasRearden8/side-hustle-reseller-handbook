# Fee-Think Worksheet

One page, pencil-grade math, designed to be used *while standing in a
thrift store*. Print it, or run it in your head, or — when the stack of
decisions gets bigger than a page — let the companion **Resale Lot
Builder** tool run the same arithmetic over your whole CSV at once.

## The thinking ladder (four rungs)

For any item you're about to pay for, fill in top to bottom:

1. **What does it usually sell for?** (Sold listings, not hopes.)
2. **What's the whole ask?** Price the buyer would pay, including shipping
   if you'd roll it into the price.
3. **What comes out?** Fees (about 13-15%, round to 15 to be safe) and the
   shipping label if you're paying it.
4. **What's left, minus cost? Is that a number worth your evening?**

## The worksheet (copy it on paper)

```text
ITEM: __________________________   DATE: _________

1. TYPICAL SOLD PRICE ................ $ _________
2. WHAT A BUYER WOULD PAY ME ......... $ _________
     (list price; or price + shipping)
3. MARKETPLACE FEES, ROUNDED ~15% .... $ _________ = (#2 x 0.15)
4. SHIPPING LABEL IF *I* PAY ......... $ _________
5. WHAT'S LEFT BEFORE ITEM COST ...... $ _________ = (#2 - #3 - #4)
6. MY ITEM COST ...................... $ _________
7. MY PROFIT IF NOTHING GOES WRONG ... $ _________ = (#5 - #6)
8. WHAT COULD GO WRONG (1 thing) ..... ________________
9. STILL WORTH IT? ...................  YES / NO / MAYBE
```

## A worked example (with deliberately round numbers)

You're looking at a sturdy, brand name, unpowered gadget for **$6**.

```text
1. TYPICAL SOLD PRICE ................ $ 30    (recent solds: 22–38, mid=30)
2. WHAT A BUYER WOULD PAY ME ......... $ 30    (free shipping promise)
3. MARKETPLACE FEES, ROUNDED ~15% .... $ 4.50
4. SHIPPING LABEL IF *I* PAY ......... $ 6     (heavier than an envelope)
5. WHAT'S LEFT BEFORE ITEM COST ...... $ 19.50
6. MY ITEM COST ...................... $ 6
7. MY PROFIT IF NOTHING GOES WRONG ... $ 13.50   ← "about $13"
8. WHAT COULD GO WRONG (1 thing) ..... knob missing → buh-bye price
9. STILL WORTH IT? ...................  YES (check the knob *before* paying)
```

Thirteen dollars of *paper* profit for one calm listing, one envelope-sized
question, one standing drop-off trip. That's a real side-hustle unit of
work. Contrast the same math on a **$2 book**:

```text
1. TYPICAL SOLD PRICE ................ $ 7
2. WHAT A BUYER WOULD PAY ME ......... $ 7
3. MARKETPLACE FEES, ROUNDED ~15% .... $ 1.05  (round up: $1.10)
4. SHIPPING LABEL IF *I* PAY ......... $ 4     (padded envelope, media-adjacent rate)
5. WHAT'S LEFT BEFORE ITEM COST ...... $ 1.90
6. MY ITEM COST ...................... $ 2
7. MY PROFIT / LOSS .................. ≈ –$0.10 ... PROBLEM
```

Why the problem line matters: at the $7 tier, the **shipping label eats the
margin** unless your sourcing cost is *effectively zero* (your own shelves,
or a "free-fill-a-bag" table) *and* your label technique is genuinely the
cheapest honest one. This is why the books-first advice always comes with
a **volume** caveat — one calm book listing is decoration; a batch of
fifteen is a category. (When you want to know if a *bundle* of those books
nets more — that's the lot-vs-single math for **Resale Lot Builder**.)

## The walk-away floor

Line 7 exists to protect you on **reprice day**. When a listing ages
([day-15 markdown](03-photos-and-listings.md)), your floor — the lowest
price you'll ever accept — is:

> **floor = your item cost + fees-at-that-price + label if you pay it**

Below the floor, "selling" is a donation to the postal service. Write the
floor on the same paper as the listing draft. If a *best-offer* arrives
above the floor, accept or counter with peace; below the floor, decline
and wait for the next buyer — there's always one, just not always quickly,
and that's what [fun checks](08-quitting-politely.md) are for.

## Worksheet, at a glance (when there's no paper)

- Under **$10 sold price**: label math will dominate. Envelope category
  only, free-source category preferred.
- **$10–$40 sold**: the Half Rule is accurate enough; cost of item barely
  matters.
- **$40+**: actually fill the worksheet every time. One line of paper has
  saved more than one weekend.
- Any price you wouldn't want to *explain* to a friend in two sentences:
  probably over-thought; walk or simplify.

The whole point of fee-think is *fewer decisions, not more.* One worksheet,
nine lines, one yes/no. Fill it once per item and stop doing math after
the register.
