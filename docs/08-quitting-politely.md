# Quitting, Politely (When It Stops Being Fun)

A side hustle is allowed to end. In fact, ending it well is a skill —
and conveniently, almost every part of a polite exit is just tidying the
good habits you already have. This chapter is the one nobody expects in a
beginner manual and it's here on purpose: *quitting on purpose is what
keeps a hobby from curdling into a burden.*

## The three-question fun check

No drama, no spreadsheet. Ask these on any given week:

1. **Did the good hours outnumber the resentful ones?** ("Resentful" is
   the feeling when you tape a box angry.)
2. **Is the money-to-sanity ratio something you'd brag about to a friend?
   Or is it "I cleared $6/hr and it cost me a Saturday"?**
3. **Would you do this weekend even if the money were kept in a jar
   uncounted?** (The pure-hobby test.)

One bad week is a *week*. Two is a season. Three in a row is data. We're
not auditing you; we're just checking whether the thing is still earning
its place. Part-time work has to pay in money **or** pleasure — the
moment it pays in neither, politely ending it is a *success metric*, not
a defeat.

## The polite wind-down (four to six weeks, calm)

Full stop in one stroke is rarely the kind move — to your ratings, your
buyers, or yourself. The gentle version:

- **Stop buying. Sooner than feels right.** The fastest de-stress in this
  whole book: none new comes in. Give your last sourcing trip to the
  [sourcing rules](02-sourcing-calmly.md) as tuition.
- **Sell through what you listed.** Let the
  [markdown clock](03-photos-and-listings.md) do its patient work; most
  of a modest stash sells in 4–6 weeks at walk-away prices.
- **Bundle what's left.** This is the chapter where the companion
  **Resale Lot Builder** earns its keep: it takes the items lying around
  and forecasts which themed lots net more sold as one listing — a
  genuinely useful final act for a shoebox's worth of remainders.
- **Donate the true remainders.** Charity shops want them; the tax form
  may even have a line for it if you itemize; and — genuinely — donated
  things are *gone*, which is the point of an exit.
- **Turn on vacation mode** (eBay: "time away" settings) rather than
  letting messages and late buyers pile up in a month you've mentally
  checked out of. Slow replies hurt ratings; an away-banner hurts nobody.
- **Close the loop for real buyers:** handle every open question
  *before* the last shipping day, so you're not the seller who vanishes.

## The records you keep after the last sale

- **Keep the [records](06-keeping-records.md) for tax year + a sensible
  quiet period** — a decent rule of thumb in round numbers is "at least
  three years of files," since tax questions rarely poke further back.
  Photos of receipts count; so does a USB stick in a drawer that closes.
- **The tax envelope gets spent or saves.** If you over-saved all year,
  the change is a graduation present to yourself.
- Note the **final numbers** somewhere human: "2026: sold $520 of stuff I
  mostly already owned; profit $340; roughly 40 calm hours; two friends
  made at the post office." That line is the story, and in a year it will
  read with more warmth than you expect.

## What the polite exit buys you

An exit done this way leaves you with:

- **No dangling obligations** — stragglers refunded, ratings intact, your
  seller name unwounded if you ever return.
- **Clean taxes** — an uneventful filing beats a dramatic one.
- **Skills that travel** — you now know how to photograph an object,
  price reality vs. hope, ship without panic, and smell a scam message
  early. Those four don't un-learn when the listings stop.
- **The freedom to come back.** People pause this hobby like a book —
  halfway, bookmarked, with the shrine of good supplies still in the
  closet. Vacation mode exists precisely for that.

## And if the answer is "not quitting, just tired"

Then it's a rhythm problem, not an exit problem. Choose one:

- **Cut the categories.** Keep the one that still feels fun; retire two.
- **Cap the pace.** One listing batch a week, shipping one standing day.
  Same system, less surface area.
- **Take a real month off** — vacation mode is longer than it looks, and
  inventory famously doesn't rot.

Halfway through a life is a fine place to have a hobby. The honest
accounting from [fees and taxes](05-fees-and-taxes.md) applies here too:
the *real* profit of a side hustle is money **plus** what it does to your
Tuesday evenings. When both columns are zeros three months running, the
fun check above already told you what you wanted — you just needed
permission to write the note, which is:

> "Thank you so much for buying from me. All my active listings are on
> vacation hold while I step back. Everything's still shipping on time
> until then — it's just time."

No shame found anywhere in this chapter. Selling a couch you found looks
suspiciously like knowing what your Saturdays are for.
