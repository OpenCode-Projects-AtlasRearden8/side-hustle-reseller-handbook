# Keeping Records (Simple, Durable, Done)

Reselling records have one job: at tax time, prove the story you already
know. They don't need to be pretty, complete to the cent, or stored in
fancy software. They need to *exist*, be findable, and get backed up
every so often. This chapter gives you the simplest version that survives
an audit-shaped afternoon with your head up.

## The one table that does everything

One spreadsheet — or one paper notebook page per month if you're analog —
with these columns:

| Date | Item | Source | Paid | Platform | Buyer paid | Fees+ship | Sold? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10/03 | 2 hardcover children's books | thrift | $3.00 | eBay | $22.00 | $4.30 | yes |
| 10/11 | watercolor pad, 9x12 | own house | $0.00 | eBay | — | — | listed |
| 10/19 | hand plane | yard sale | $8.00 | eBay | $58.00 | $9.75 | yes |
| *TOTAL (this month)* | | | *$11.00* | | *$80.00* | *$14.05* | |

Every tax-relevant question falls out of seven columns. When digital
becomes annoying, a monthly **photograph of the notebook page** is an
acceptable backup method — blurry is fine; receipts live in a second
photo album if you bought from a store.

## The monthly twenty minutes

Once a month, the whole bookkeeping routine:

1. **Copy totals into the month row.** Your marketplace's *Payments /
   Statements* page lists the total from the year, but copy your own
   running total into a **second tab** (or a total line at the bottom).
2. **Envelope the tax share.** From the
   [25%-of-profit rule](05-fees-and-taxes.md), move the number to the
   cushion.
3. **Photograph receipts** from the month into one folder / album named
   `resale-receipts-2026-10`. Done means *backed up*, not organized.
4. **Close the month.** Two totals to scribble: money-in, money-spent.
   Anything else is decoration.

Twenty minutes. If you find yourself spending an evening on it, the system
grew; prune it back to the seven columns.

## What counts as an expense (plain-language version)

Worth remembering, in rounded plain English — *keep the receipts for these*:

- What you paid for inventory (thrift receipts, store receipts, the
  shipping counter).
- Marketplace and payment fees (they show up in your account statements,
  downloadable as PDF or CSV — no manual tracking needed).
- Packing supplies, tape, scale: real, small, keep receipts.
- The mileage part of sourcing trips and post-office trips is a category
  worth one look at tax time, not a hobby today (a small **mileage log**
  app or one paper line per trip does until then).
- A portion of your home internet or a room *can* count under stricter
  rules — this is squarely "ask a professional when it grows" territory.

The technique behind the receipts: **photograph every paper receipt the
day you get it.** Paper fades; camera rolls don't.

## What the records *protect*

When you keep the seven columns, you don't need intent to be perfect when:

- You file taxes — profit is one subtraction, receipts exist for the best
  scary-questions version of the meeting.
- You buy a **bigger** thing — because a growing hustle needs a paper
  trail of exactly the shape you already built.
- [A buyer claims something went wrong](07-scam-proofing.md) — the row
  with the sale date and weight is your whole defense exhibit.
- You [quit politely](08-quitting-politely.md) — the winding-down story
  ("sold, donated, kept") is present in your own handwriting.

## Backups, the low-energy version

The spreadsheet lives in **two places** from day one: your local Documents
folder *and* the one cloud you already pay occasional attention to (or any
USB stick in your bag). Backup cadence for a part-timer: **when you close
the month**, copy the file over. If a laptop dies, you lose — at absolute
worst — the current month's rows. That's a *win*, not a disaster.

Exactly one rule to obey: **never keep the only copy inside the app that
would disappear with it.** If a reselling app or marketplace dashboard is
where your list of items lives, copy it into your own file — once a month
is plenty.

## When you outgrow the notebook

Signs you're ready for real bookkeeping software — which is a *good*
problem: real store receipts showing up at volume, more platforms than
fingers, first business-bank-account question, or a professional asking.
Until then, one table, seven columns, a photo of every receipt, a monthly
envelope — this is the whole system, and it holds a part-time hustle
comfortably for years.
